# Webinar Script — "Own Your Stack" (~12 minutes)

**What this is:** the full spoken-word script for the ~12-minute webinar that WhatsApp group members are funneled into, with timestamps and B-roll markers.
**How to use it:** record it straight through; keep the 12 rapid demos to ~30 seconds each. Every price matches the README table and `src/sales-data.js` — re-verify before recording, and re-record the deal-mechanics section whenever the pricing model changes. Replace `[brackets]` with real values.

---

## 0:00–2:00 — The subscription problem

[B-ROLL: screen recording of a bank statement / subscription tracker, monthly charges scrolling past: Loom, Calendly, Typeform, Buffer, Otter, FreshBooks, Linktree, SmallPDF]

> Let's do some quick math together. Not hypothetical math — your math.
>
> A screen recorder: Loom, $15 a month — that's $180 a year. A booking link: Calendly, $120 a year. A form builder: Typeform, $348 a year. Social scheduling: Buffer, about $360 a year for five channels. Transcription: Otter, $204 a year. Invoicing: FreshBooks, $228 a year. A link-in-bio: Linktree, $84 a year. A PDF tool: SmallPDF, $144 a year.
>
> [B-ROLL: running total ticking up on screen: 180 → 300 → 648 → 1,008 → 1,212 → 1,440 → 1,524 → 1,668]
>
> That's eight completely ordinary tools. Nothing exotic — the kind of thing every freelancer, creator, and small team has. **$1,668. Every year. Forever.** And those prices only move in one direction.
>
> Here's the part that got me: none of these tools are new. A screen recorder is not a breakthrough in [current year]. You're not paying for innovation — you're paying rent. And the moment you stop paying, you lose the tool and, in a lot of cases, your data with it.
>
> So I decided to do something slightly unreasonable about it.

## 2:00–8:00 — Twelve apps in six minutes

> I'm building 102 apps that replace the most common subscriptions. You pay once, you own it forever. Desktop apps run entirely on your machine — no accounts, no telemetry. Web apps self-host on a five-dollar VPS you control. And every single one has a free MIT-licensed version on GitHub, so you never have to take my word for anything. [Note: WisperTalk is the one exception — paid and closed-source; say "almost every" if you demo it.]
>
> Here are twelve of them. Thirty seconds each. Go.

[B-ROLL: for each app — 5 seconds of the app icon + name card, then ~20 seconds of real screen recording of the actual app in use, price line at the bottom the whole time]

**3:00 — Captionly.** Replaces Submagic. Local Whisper auto-captions with karaoke word highlighting, burned in with ffmpeg — nothing uploaded. Submagic runs about $216 a year. Captionly is **$34, once**.

**3:30 — Bookslot.** Replaces Calendly. Your own booking page — timezone-correct, with email and calendar invites, on your own server. Calendly is $120 a year per user. Bookslot is **$99, once**.

**4:00 — Linkleaf.** Replaces Linktree. A self-hosted link-in-bio with themes, link scheduling, email capture and analytics. Linktree charges up to $84 a year. Linkleaf is **$19, once** — and it's your domain, your data.

**4:30 — Formforge.** Replaces Typeform. Beautiful forms with unlimited responses, file uploads and CSV export — self-hosted. Typeform is $348 a year. Formforge is **$99, once**. Unlimited is the point.

**5:00 — PostDock.** Replaces Buffer. Social scheduling with a composer, queue, content recycling and webhooks — your API keys, your data. Buffer runs about $360 a year for five channels. PostDock is **$129, once**.

**5:30 — Billcraft.** Replaces FreshBooks. Clients, invoices, PDFs, share links and recurring billing — on hardware you control. FreshBooks is $228 a year. Billcraft is **$99, once**.

**6:00 — Upwatch.** Replaces UptimeRobot. Uptime checks, an incident log, alerts, and a branded 90-day status page — on your own box. UptimeRobot is about $96 a year. Upwatch is **$99, once**.

**6:30 — PDFsmith.** Replaces SmallPDF. Merge, split, rotate, watermark, convert — offline. Your documents never get uploaded to anyone's server. SmallPDF is about $144 a year. PDFsmith is **$29, once**.

**7:00 — WhisperDesk.** Replaces Otter.ai. Whisper transcription on your own CPU — unlimited minutes, exports TXT, SRT and VTT. Otter is $204 a year. WhisperDesk is **$39, once**. Unlimited minutes, again, is the point.

**7:30 — Cutaway.** Replaces remove.bg. Local AI background removal — unlimited images, full resolution, nothing uploaded. remove.bg is about $108 a year for 40 credits a month. Cutaway is **$24, once**, no credits, no caps.

>
> You can buy any app individually, any time, at onetimesuite.com/apps/ — the prices I just said, that's it, done.
>
> Or you can buy the whole suite — every app, including everything still to ship — as one bundle. And the bundle has a pricing model you've probably never seen: **it started at $97, and every single sale raises the price by one dollar.** Today it sits at **[current price from sales-data.js — check /price-tracker/]**.
>
> During the 100-day build, the price is capped at **$997** — it can never go above that while I'm still shipping. Once all the apps are done, the bundle becomes **$1,997** and the counter stops.
>
> So the deal is simple and it's honest: the earlier you buy, the less you pay, and the price only ever moves up. No fake countdown timers, no "sale ending soon" that restarts next week. The counter is real, it's public on the price-tracker page, and every buyer after you pays more than you did.
